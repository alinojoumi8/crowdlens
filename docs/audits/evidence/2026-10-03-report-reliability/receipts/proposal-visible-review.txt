Four named participants and their attributed positions

The source identifies four participants, each representing a distinct stakeholder lens:

Maya Chen (resident cyclist) — supports the pilot conditional on protected intersections; reports currently avoiding Main Street because traffic feels unsafe.
Leo Martin (Main Street shop owner) — concerned about the loss of short-stay parking; could support a trial if loading access remains reliable and customer visits are measured.
Priya Shah (resident with limited mobility) — requires that accessible pickup spaces and a clear pedestrian path remain; states that a bike lane alone does not answer those needs.
Daniel Brooks (city transport planner) — frames the pilot as reversible and commits to publishing counts of cyclists, deliveries, customer visits and accessibility complaints before any permanence decision is considered.
This participatory composition — cyclist, shop owner, accessibility advocate, transport planner — is the source's own framing and explains why safety, parking/loading, accessibility and measurement all appear as legitimate concerns from the outset.

# Cedarbrook bike-lane pilot — fictional browser test

This is a fictional example for testing CrowdLens. Cedarbrook and the people below are invented. It is not a real municipal proposal or a prediction.

Cedarbrook proposes an eight-week protected bike-lane pilot on Main Street. One parking lane would become a bike lane. Deliveries would use two signed loading bays. The city has not decided whether the pilot will become permanent.

Four participants provided these statements:
- Maya Chen, a resident who cycles to work: "I support the pilot if the intersections are protected. I currently avoid Main Street because traffic feels unsafe."
- Leo Martin, a Main Street shop owner: "I am concerned about losing short-stay parking. I could support a trial if loading access remains reliable and customer visits are measured."
- Priya Shah, a resident with limited mobility: "Accessible pickup spaces and a clear pedestrian path must remain. A bike lane alone does not answer those needs."
- Daniel Brooks, the city's transport planner: "The pilot is reversible. We will publish counts of cyclists, deliveries, customer visits and accessibility complaints before considering a permanent change."

The four statements are qualitative and do not represent all residents. There is no survey, crash count, business revenue measurement or final decision in this source. Do not invent those facts. Simulated conversations are generated output, not new documentary evidence.
